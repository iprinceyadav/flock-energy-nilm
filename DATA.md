## File Format

### Raw Data (`data/raw/`)
- **Format**: CSV files, split into Part1 and Part2 per household.
- **Part1**: Unavailable sensors recorded as `0`.
- **Part2**: Unavailable sensors recorded as `NaN`.
- **Columns**: `Time, Unix, Aggregate, Appliance1, ..., Appliance9`
- **Timestamp**: Local UK time (includes BST offsets — **not corrected** in raw data).
- **Total**: 40 CSV files (~200–270 MB each).

### Cleaned Data (`data/cleaned/`)
- **Format**: Single CSV per household.
- **Columns**: `Time, Unix, Aggregate, Appliance1, ..., Appliance9, Issues`
- **Timestamp**: Corrected for UK daylight savings (UTC-aligned).
- **Cleaning applied**: Forward-fill of NaN, spikes > 4000 W removed, IAM column re-alignment, Issues flag column added.
- **Issues column**: Set to 1 when sum of IAMs exceeds aggregate (data should be treated with caution).
- **Total**: 20 CSV files (~250–440 MB each).

## Data Quality Decisions

### Duplicate Timestamps
- **Strategy**: Keep the **last** reading at each duplicate timestamp.
- **Rationale**: Later readings at the same timestamp likely represent more recent sensor polls.

### Missing Data
- **Short gaps (≤ 3 minutes)**: Linear interpolation.
- **Long outages (> 15 minutes)**: Left as NaN, flagged with `is_outage=1`.
- **Rationale**: 3-minute gaps correspond to ~22 missed 8-second readings — sensor polling delays of this length are normal. Longer gaps indicate genuine equipment or connectivity failures and should not be fabricated.

### Extreme Readings
- **Threshold**: > 4000 W (consistent with official cleaned dataset spike removal).
- **Action**: Replaced with NaN (not dropped, to preserve timestamp alignment).
- **Rationale**: IAMs measure individual appliances; readings above 4 kW from a single household appliance channel are almost certainly measurement errors.

### Negative Readings
- **Action**: Clipped to 0.
- **Rationale**: Active power measurements from IAMs cannot be negative; negative values indicate sensor errors.

### Resampling
- **Method**: Mean aggregation from ~8 s → 1 minute.
- **Rationale**: The source readings represent instantaneous power samples. Averaging within each minute produces 1-minute average power in watts. This is **not** an energy summation — the unit remains watts.
- **Energy conversion**: When computing energy, we use: `Energy (kWh) = Power (W) × 1 min / 60,000`.

### Remaining Concerns
1. The `Issues` flag in cleaned data indicates periods where IAM sum exceeds aggregate — these are used but flagged.
2. Appliance swaps during the study period (documented in README) may affect some channels.
3. "Television Site" and "Computer Site" may include multiple devices.
4. February 2014 has known widespread outages across many households.
